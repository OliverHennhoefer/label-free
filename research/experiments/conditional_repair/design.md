# Experiment 01 · Does deleting an alert repair a feature relationship?

Written before running the selector or either synthetic label assignment. The four ADBench development sources and their baseline results were already open. They serve as a transfer diagnostic. This experiment spends zero validation or test queries.

## Claim and rival explanation

Suppose most observations obey a stable, approximately linear relationship between measured variables. A small set of discordant rows can bias its estimated slope. If a detector flags those rows, removing them from a training sample should improve prediction of individual features on typical, untouched rows. The proposed detector-level quantity is the change in that prediction loss. A different explanation is that simply ranking detectors by the prediction errors of their alerts would give the same choices. Feature prediction for *detecting* anomalous rows has substantial prior art. [Noto, Brodley and Slonim, FRaC](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22639542/)

For example, in a centered two-feature population with normal relation `y ≈ x`, a minority following `y ≈ −x` reduces the fitted covariance. With comparable scales the ridge slope is `cov(x,y) / (var(x) + λ)`. If removing the minority moves that slope closer to the normal slope, typical normal prediction errors should decrease. A rare but valid observation far along `y ≈ x` can be informative for the slope. If ground truth calls that operating regime anomalous, the same repair preference can select the wrong detector. This argument motivates a sign prediction under conditions. It supplies no general ordering of detectors by AP.

## Measurement fixed in advance

- Use three shuffled folds with seed 1729. For each fold, standardize on all training rows. Keep these scales fixed for every deletion and predictor. For every feature, predict it linearly from the other features with a free intercept and ridge penalty 0.05 in covariance units. The per-row loss is mean squared prediction error across features.
- For each candidate, remove its highest-scoring 5% of training rows, rounding up and sharing membership across boundary ties. Compare against four random permutations of that weight pattern. Share the permutations across candidates (seed 1730). Matching the full pattern also matches the effective training size for tied scores. Score the candidate by the median across all held-out rows of the paired difference `mean random-deletion loss − candidate-deletion loss`. A positive value means improvement on the typical row. Every candidate faces the same unfiltered evaluation rows. Only the auxiliary predictions are cross-fitted. The PyOD rankings belong to the original transductive batch. Both methods abstain below two features or five rows.
- The direct-residual rival uses the same features, folds and ridge model. Fit on all training rows and give each detector the mean out-of-fold loss of its top 5% of alerts. Larger means preferred for both methods. This is a simple related comparator with a precisely matched prediction model.

## Predictions and decisions

1. **Mechanism.** Use seeds 0 through 19. In each batch of 600, generate 540 ordinary rows near `y = x`, 30 discordant rows near `(±2, ∓2)`, and 30 valid extreme rows near `(±4, ±4)`, with balanced signs in both rare groups and Gaussian response noise of standard deviation 0.15. Give both selectors the same two rankings, `|y − x|` and `|(x + y)/2|`. We predict that repair strictly prefers the first ranking in at least 16 of 20 seeds and that its repair relative to random deletion is positive in at least 16. Either failure retires this specification as a demonstration of its own mechanism. The count is a pilot decision threshold.
2. **Identifiability.** Keep each realized feature matrix and both score vectors fixed. Grade twice, first treating discordance as anomalous, then treating the extremes as anomalous. We predict that the AP-optimal ranking reverses in all 20 cases, with exactly the same label-free choices. These are two plausible meanings for the rare groups and one observation available to the selector.
3. **Rival.** The direct-residual rival should also pick the discordance ranking on at least 16 seeds. If both succeed, this constructed example supplies no evidence that deletion adds useful information. Judge the actual selected detectors on the open bank, and keep the direct comparator for any later experiment.
4. **Transfer.** Freeze both selectors on the existing four-source bank, then grade once. Report paired AP and per-source differences against default Isolation Forest and the direct rival, plus AUROC, precision at the 5% budget, ties and failures. Compare with the strongest fixed configuration already observed (IForest/64), HITS, S_Dbw and the previously tried pooled-agreement selector. No ADBench source was identified in advance as having the proposed semantics. A gain here is exploratory. If repair fails to beat both the default and the direct rival in mean AP, stop this version before fresh-source evaluation. Passing this screen would justify a new preregistered discriminating test, not a publication claim.
