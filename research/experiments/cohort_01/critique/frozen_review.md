# Independent checks of the frozen submissions

**Reflection reviewed 2026-11-22 before the transfer grade.** The implementation rotates the partition, orientation and measurement folds as specified, pools ties at common thresholds and falls back according to the frozen signed-mass gate. Two limitations affect the interpretation.

**The physical center is necessary.** Let the observed measurement be uniform on the integers 0 through 19. With physical center 9, the normal subdistribution occupies 0 through 18 uniformly and the 5% at 19 are one-sided faults. With physical center 10, the normal subdistribution occupies 1 through 19 uniformly and the 5% at 0 are one-sided faults. Every assumption about nominal symmetry, prevalence and one-sided fault support holds in both worlds. The endpoint detectors exchange AP 1.00 and 0.05. The median convention that averages the two middle observations gives 9.5 for the equally weighted observation set. Reflection about 9.5 finds exactly zero excess. The physical center cannot be recovered from this marginal distribution alone.

Arias-Castro and Jiang already prove the maximal symmetric subdensity formula and include a procedure for choosing the center by maximizing symmetric background mass. On this example its population objective is maximized at 9.5. Xiang et al. estimate reflected density ratios under a symmetric null at zero and explicitly target a surrogate local false-discovery rate. The plausible incremental contribution here is AP evaluation of fixed candidate ranks and its cross-fitted estimator under a *supplied* valid center. The cohort bank does not contain that measurement prior. [Arias-Castro and Jiang, Theorem 1 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.13925) · [Xiang et al., 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11548)

**Independent orientation leaves a finite-cell bias.** Condition on a correct center, a fixed partition, and a non-tied orientation chosen in the independent fold. Under an exactly symmetric null let either side of an oriented cell pair have probability $u$. In a measurement fold of $k$ draws let $M$ and $C$ be the counts on the chosen side and its companion. The row weights sum to $(M-C)\,\mathbf{1}_{M>0}$ for this pair. When $M=0$, the negative contribution $-C$ has no row to receive it. Its null expectation is therefore $E[C\,\mathbf{1}_{M=0}]=ku(1-u)^{k-1}>0$. For $u=1/k$ this is about 0.37 counts per oriented pair. More generally a random center estimated on the shared partition fold can induce agreement in the other folds' orientations under a physically symmetric null. A subsequent diagnostic on the same null samples changed only the center and obtained mean excess estimates separated by 9.75 percentage points (9.89% and 0.14%). This isolates a substantial centering effect. The empty-cell bias remains an analytic contribution whose magnitude in those samples is unmeasured. **Radial minimax regret reviewed 2026-11-22.** The AP numerator in the code is the sum of each score group's risk increment times the cumulative risk through that score, divided by the number of rows through that score. This respects ties. The hard-cutoff rival uses the diagonal profile terms from the same radius and thresholds. The rank-one risk products are relevant to *population* AP with stochastic row outcomes. A single observed labeling produces a random empirical ratio.

Rounding the seven proposed masses on six rows gives four distinct hard cutoffs at 1, 2, 3 and 6 rows. The 258,840 unordered strict-ranking pairs reduce to 7,140 distinct pairs of profile types. After the predeclared 1/20 grid screen, the inventor optimized over all faces of the continuous four-profile simplex for every pair and found zero opposite strict minimax choices. That exhausts this six-row decision under the specified bands. It leaves opportunities for larger supports, other profiles or portfolios of more than two detectors. A separate six-cutoff example reverses a weak ordering of candidate AP at all deterministic cutoffs when the risk becomes a particular soft calibration. This establishes a failure of inferring fixed-calibration AP order from hard-cutoff dominance. The registered minimax action still has no strict reversal on its four-profile experiment. Withholding follows the frozen decision. Maximizing a pair's performance gap over unknown relevance has direct information-retrieval prior art, including AP. Tan and Clarke optimize over relevance assignments. The proposed restriction to mixtures of ordered radial risks and a minimax choice among candidate detectors is the distinct part that would need a consequential witness. An observed radius distribution alone cannot supply the premise that the anomaly risk depends only on radius. [Tan and Clarke, 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.3587)
