# Can reflected measurements identify the detector with higher AP?

Recorded before running the simulations or either selector. The four original ADBench sources were open. On Ionosphere seed 0, S_Dbw found the AP-leading KNN/5, while HITS selected default Isolation Forest. Either consensus or geometric isolation could reward valid, infrequent observations. A different source of evidence is available when the measurement process supplies nominal symmetry.

## Assumptions and estimand

Consider measurements centered at a known baseline and an involution R that changes the sign of every centered coordinate. Let the observed probability measure have density p and let a be the joint density of observations and positive labels. The nominal subdensity p − a is invariant to R. If faults occur on at most one side of each orbit, then a(x) = max(p(x) − p(Rx), 0). Its integral is the anomaly rate. The same equality applies to paired cells of a partition when each cell pair has faults on at most one side. Within an enriched cell we additionally need the positive fraction to be constant given the candidate's score, or the cell to resolve the fault probability adequately. This makes the observed fraction of positives in every score tail, hence population non-interpolated average precision, identifiable. This is a population functional. Plugging posterior weights into empirical AP does not give the exact finite-sample expectation of a ratio of random variables.

The maximal symmetric-background decomposition already has a proof, and reflected-density ratios have already been used for local false discovery rates. Testing real observations against reflected ones also has precedent. Our question concerns estimating AP for externally supplied detectors, including the effects of choosing the enriched side and measuring its excess on the same sample. [Arias-Castro and Jiang, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.13925) · [Xiang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11548) · [Tombs and Lester](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05442)

Calibration at a symmetric operating point and signed sensor measurements could justify reflection in deployment. Paired fault-injection or independently collected nominal runs could check it. The frozen benchmark has neither. Its implementation substitutes a coordinatewise median for the baseline and assumes counts in its paired cells approximate the physical symmetry. A positive imbalance cannot establish the nominal symmetry, the anomaly semantics or the one-sidedness condition.

## Frozen measurement

Split the rows into three shuffled folds with random seed 9137. Use one fold to estimate the center and fit a centrally symmetric Voronoi partition under the Euclidean distance in the supplied standardized coordinates. Include the fixed origin cell and approximately the square root of half the partition-training count in nonzero paired prototypes, capped at 32. Initialize by distance-squared sampling with seed 9138 plus the partition-fold index. Update each prototype by aligning its assigned training rows to its sign and averaging. Use at most 12 updates. Keep the partition fixed for the other two folds.

In one fold compare the counts in the two cells of each pair to choose the enriched side. In the other, give rows on that side signed weight (its count − its mirror count) / its count. Set weights to zero for unpopulated cells, ties in the orientation fold, and the fixed cell. For a candidate threshold, summing weights estimates its positive mass under the within-cell assumption. Nominal sampling noise on the chosen side can now contribute with either sign. Swap the last two folds and rotate all three roles. Average the two weights available per row. Accumulate score ties together in non-interpolated, signed plug-in AP. Keep the raw estimated prevalence and AP for diagnostics. Abstain if the mean signed weight is at most 2 / sqrt(n), or exceeds 0.5, or if fewer than 30 rows are available. These are pilot screening thresholds. They carry no confidence or validity guarantee.

The matched rival uses identical centers, cells, scoring and gate. It chooses the enriched side on the same fold that supplies the count difference, producing the usual clipped positive excess in every cell pair. Also compare with a geometry-only rival using AP against the top 5% of rows by leave-one-out distance to their ceiling-square-root(n)-th observed neighbor, with fractional cutoffs. A score-only selector faces exactly the same candidate matrix in each paired world.

## Predictions and stopping rules

1. **Companion-swap test.** Generate 24 seeded pairs of 2,000-row measurements. Four sign triples with positive product define a tetrahedron. Candidate A raises all positive-product vertices on the inner shell and B raises those on the outer shell. Each shell contributes 12% of all rows on these vertices, using identical translated jitter. Put 12% on the reflected vertices of the valid shell and 2% on those of the active shell. The remaining 62% form a symmetric central baseline. Swap which shell has the missing companions, while retaining the same alert rows and candidate score vectors. The nominal subdistribution in each world balances each orbit. On the excess labeling, prevalence is 0.10 and the two population APs are 5/6 for the active candidate and 0.10 for the other. The split rule should strictly pick the correct candidate in both worlds for at least 20/24 paired replicates. The geometry-only rival should win at most 60% of worlds, counting ties at one half. Record mean absolute errors of the prevalence and both APs. An average absolute prevalence error above 0.04 retires the calibration claim even if the choices succeed.
2. **Noise-only test of the split.** Generate 24 independent iid null batches of 2,000 rows with 12% probability in each shell/sign group and 52% in the symmetric central baseline. Mean absolute signed prevalence should be below 0.04 and below half the mean clipped estimate. The split gate should abstain in at least 20/24 batches. A failure retires the proposed noise correction. The split and clipped scores may both pick the right detector under a strong signal.
3. **Failure with exactly the same prevalence and observables.** Retain every feature and score in each companion-swap world. Add balanced positive mass of 0.24 either in the central baseline or in both hemispheres of the valid shell. The resulting prevalence is 0.34 in both labelings. Both retain a symmetric nominal population. We predict opposite AP-optimal candidates for every pair of labelings. Thus even an oracle for the true prevalence and the nominal symmetry cannot recover the AP winner without some restriction on hidden symmetric faults.
4. **Transfer.** Submit the split rule and its clipped control before any fresh ADBench grades. Report mean source-block AP including abstentions, coverage, ties and material losses for all sources against the clipped control, IForest/64, default IForest and the referee's other baselines. A general-selection lead requires both the paired synthetic test and higher all-source mean AP than the clipped control, a strong fixed detector and the strongest relevant benchmark comparison. There is no prospectively grounded claim that an ADBench source has the signed sensor semantics. Coverage conditional on the gate is a secondary diagnostic.

One pair of independently generated operating populations is one mechanism replicate, and one iid null batch is one noise replicate. The unit of benchmark transfer is the source. A fresh-source grade tests the frozen rule once.
