# Detector choice when an instrument orders severity but does not calibrate it

Recorded on 2026-11-22 before any synthetic experiment, new public-bank predictions, or cohort grades for this arm. The open Pima example motivates the endpoint. Its best detector under AP differed from its best detector under AUROC on both seeds. An ordering of rows alone does not give the AP of an imperfect approximation to that ordering.

## Decision and prospective scope

Choose one fixed candidate from a supplied portfolio to minimize its worst possible **population AP regret** relative to the best candidate at each admissible calibration. A deployment must supply an engineering or domain justification that anomaly probability increases with a known severity measurement and depends on the features only through that measurement. One example is a multichannel deviation alarm whose faults depend on the energy of a perturbation in background scatter units, with unknown escalation probabilities. For this cohort the severity measurement is sample squared Mahalanobis radius. Shared center and scatter shape, a uniform direction independent of radius, and a finite second radial moment make the population covariance proportional to the common scatter. These are sufficient conditions to recover the ordering in the population. The decision rule also applies to directly known severity scores. Diagnosis, mode membership, directional faults, and very heavy-tailed background can break the radius model. The unlabeled batch cannot certify its label semantics.

The finite support comprises the observed rows with equal mass, with actual conditional anomaly risk constant within radial bands and increasing across their boundaries. The boundaries are the upper 1/64, 1/32, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2 and 1 of the rows, rounded up to whole rows. These predetermined dyadic masses cover rare and broad tails. Equal radii share fractional boundary membership. Any convex mixture of these seven nested upper-tail membership profiles is allowed, together with arbitrary common scaling from zero to one. This includes background risk and stochastic transitions. There is no prior over calibration, no assumed prevalence, and no subgroup of named benchmark datasets. The all-batch ADBench AP comparison is a **transport test**. Advance a general-selection lead only if mean source-level AP beats both the identical-radius hard-threshold control and IForest/64 on the six new sources. Report default Isolation Forest, pooled agreement, HITS, S_Dbw and all source harms as the cohort requires. A method tied with the matched control cannot attribute a gain to calibration uncertainty. No automatic test of the scientific scope is proposed.

## Calculation and nearest rival

For candidate score groups in decreasing order let `b_g` be the vector of positive masses contributed by group `g` under each threshold profile and `n_g` the total cumulative mass of rows through that group. Let `p` be the vector of profile prevalences and `w` be the simplex of profile increments. Non-interpolated AP on the population equals `w' Q w / (p' w)`, where `Q` symmetrizes the sum of outer products `b_g (sum_{j<=g} b_j)' / n_g`. This calculates AP of the population with those row-conditional risks. A single stochastic labeling of a finite batch has random empirical AP. In particular, multiplying risks does not give the finite-sample expectation of that random ratio.

For each ordered pair of candidates, maximize the difference of these rational quadratics over the simplex. Compare normalized quadratic forms on all nonempty faces. The denominator is `w' A w` for `A_ij=(p_i+p_j)/2` on the simplex. At an interior stationary point of a face, `(Q_difference - r A)w = 0`. Among globally maximizing weights take one with smallest support. If the nullspace on that face had dimension at least two, one could move to its boundary while keeping the same objective. Its one-dimensional nullspace and `w' A w > 0` give a simple finite generalized eigenvalue. Vertices and positive finite eigenvectors on the faces therefore suffice. Common risk scaling preserves comparisons and scales regret, so the worst case is attained at full scale. The action minimizes maximum regret among **deterministic choices**. This claim does not solve a lottery over detectors.

The matched rival sees the same radial information, score ties, and candidate portfolio. It minimizes maximum AP regret over the seven individual hard thresholds. Its search omits mixtures of thresholds. This is stronger than using one arbitrary cutoff and isolates whether the soft calibration model changes an action. Also record how frequently the action differs from simply maximizing Spearman agreement with radius or AP using one top-5% radius label. The latter two are descriptive checks.

## Predictions and stopping rules

1. On a six-support population with different severities, enumerate all pairs of strict rankings. The unit is a distinct unordered pair of the 720 possible candidate rankings. A rational simplex grid with increments of 1/20 gives an independent lower bound on worst regret. I predict a pair with a strictly different minimax choice, and a worst-case population AP-regret improvement of at least **0.01** over the hard-threshold-only choice. Report the full search denominator, fraction of distinct strict choices on the grid, and an exact continuous-solver check of the strongest grid witness. Solver output for that witness must cover the independent grid and match direct supervised AP on a replicated labeled support for the selected rational grid profile. Count tied choices separately. Report this as an existence test and make no general frequency claim about deployed portfolios.
2. Failure control uses exactly the same 16 concentric rings, eight equally spaced directions per ring, two scores and prevalence 1/8. Label either the outer two rings or one direction as anomalous. The radius-ordered candidate should win under the radial mechanism and lose under the angular mechanism, with at least 0.5 AP regret in the angular control. That rules out inferring the severity semantics from the observed geometry or score bank.
3. Check tied score groups, degenerate radii, and runtime on an unlabeled 2,000 by 64 batch with eight candidates. Include the four existing public development banks only for forward-path feasibility and choice comparisons. No supervisory grading is performed here. If the finite-support example fails the 0.01 threshold, record failure of the substantive prediction even if tiny improvements exist. Submit one method and the matched control if the exact optimization is valid and a strict decision difference is verified. Freeze before any evaluation on additional real sources.

## Nearest primary work

Aslam, Pavlu and Yilmaz write binary AP as a weighted sum of relevance products to construct a sampling estimator. Our question uses the population version of this identity to compare detectors over a partially identified calibration. [A Sampling Technique for Efficiently Estimating Measures of Query Retrieval Performance](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/jaa/papers/AslamPaYi05.pdf)

Manski develops minimax-regret decisions under incomplete knowledge in treatment selection. The decision criterion predates this proposal. [Statistical Treatment Rules for Heterogeneous Populations](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2004.00530.x)

Mahalanobis supplies the distance used in this sensor instantiation. Clémençon and Robbiano connect density level sets to supervised anomaly ranking with a uniform reference. Neither supplies evidence that a particular clinical or ADBench label is radial. [Mahalanobis, On the Generalised Distance in Statistics](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13171-019-00164-5) · [Clémençon and Robbiano, Anomaly Ranking as Supervised Bipartite Ranking](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/clemencon14.html)
