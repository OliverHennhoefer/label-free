# Running a first research loop

The decision is which detector to trust when every real example available for fitting and selection is known to be normal. The hidden question is how well the chosen detector will rank real outliers in a separate test. A contribution can improve an existing estimate or introduce a different observable. A result can be valuable under a specified data-generating process. For example, two detectors may agree on every normal record and behave differently beyond the observed range. What justified assumption about future faults would favor one of them?

## A brief to give a research agent

> Investigate one uncertainty about detector selection from normal data alone. Use a public bank whose `track` is `normal-only`. The trusted evaluator keeps mixed test rows, their scores and their labels private. Record any artificial probes a method derives from the normal observations and the assumption they encode. Name the supervised decision that matters and read the closest primary work before claiming a contribution. You can start with an existing mathematical target, a generative model, a measurement process, or a counterexample. You are free to conclude that the available observations leave the choice unresolved.
>
> Describe what would have to be true for an observable quantity to order two detectors by the chosen utility. What could produce the same observation with a different best detector? Find a rival explanation that could survive the obvious demonstration. Choose an experiment on which the explanations make different predictions. If you improve an estimator, compare each estimate with the known population value somewhere. If you introduce a new signal, remove or change its hypothesized cause while preserving a plausible shortcut. Use the cheapest experiment that can distinguish the claims.
>
> Before looking at new grades, save the prediction, counterexample, information available at selection time, comparison, unit of replication and what outcome would change your mind. Implement the smallest experiment that tests the claim. Run it, freeze any selector on the public bank, then request its development grade. Explain wins and harms at the source level, including ties and abstentions. Leave a short record of what changed in your understanding and one unresolved question whose answer would change the next experiment. An informative failure completes a turn.

The handoff for the completed first turn is in the [experiment record](experiments/conditional_repair/result.md). The [reference-mass example](experiments/reference_mass/README.md) explores the alternative of improving an existing estimand. These examples illustrate two choices an investigator made. The next hypothesis can originate elsewhere.

## What an agent can learn from the historical mixed-data evidence

- **Supervised metrics describe different decisions.** AUROC measures the probability that a positive outranks a negative, with half credit for ties. AP averages precision across increments in recall. Precision at 5% asks which rows receive a finite alert budget. The fixed-detector entries in a development report reveal their oracle choices. On both Pima seeds here, HBOS/10 maximized AP (0.572) and KNN/25 maximized AUROC (0.719). Choosing the endpoint after seeing the result would change the question.
- **Start with picks and counterexamples.** On Ionosphere seed 0, S_Dbw picked the AP winner, KNN/5. HITS picked default Isolation Forest. On Cardio seed 0, default Isolation Forest was itself the AP winner. For each case, ask what property the selector rewards and what other data-generating process could give the same signal. A method's mean AP or Spearman correlation alone cannot explain those choices. The per-task evidence is in `by_task` in the development report. [Initial audit and primary literature](../benchmarks/RESEARCH_NOTES.md)
- **Separate a measurement failure from a target failure.** With a synthetic population of known geometry, compute the exact mass or volume and compare it with the library estimate. Then ask whether even the exact quantity chooses the best detector for the particular anomaly mechanism. Those two errors lead to different research programs. [Worked example and prior art](experiments/reference_mass/README.md)

## Evidence for a scoped claim

The researcher can use released development grades to learn which hypotheses failed. A deployed selector sees known-normal examples only. Record any historical labels, semantic descriptions, external fault models or reference samples used to design or apply a method. An advantage confined to one named benchmark dataset is a lead. Define the mechanism and its scope prospectively, then test independent source groups that could have contradicted it. If applicability is determined from the data, count the cost and errors of that decision too.

Start with one question and one development grade of frozen alternatives. Compare with the default, the development-chosen fixed detector, and the closest existing label-free method using identical tasks. Report the chosen detector's utility, regret and harms. Average repeated seeds within source groups. When the observation does not discriminate between rival mechanisms, design a different test or stop the line. A change in a weight, threshold or family name needs a scientific motivation of its own.

For a blind run, give the inventor the public bank and only the agreed feedback. A trusted grader holds private files and any raw ADBench labels. The local runner snapshots code and does not supply OS isolation. Freeze the claim, endpoint, subgroup definition, comparisons and allowed looks before consulting validation. Reserve test and independent replication for the publication candidate.
