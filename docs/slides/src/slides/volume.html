Level-set volume

How little volume does the detector need to enclose most of the data?

Two regions, each holding 90% of the data

Volume needed against mass enclosed. Lower is better.

IdeaThe ideal score is the data density. Its level sets are the smallest regions that hold a given mass.

MetricsMass-Volume MV(α): smallest volume holding mass α. Excess-Mass EM(t): largest mass − t · volume.

Fails whenMore than a few dimensions: volume is estimated from uniform samples.

If anomalies are the points in low-density regions, the best possible score is the density itself, and its level sets are minimum-volume sets. So the volume a detector needs is a label-free measure of how close it is to that ideal (Clémençon and Jacob 2013; Goix, Sabourin, Clémençon 2015). Every score threshold defines a region. A good detector holds 90% of the data in a small region that follows the clusters (blue); a poor one needs a large region that includes empty space (grey). MV(α) is the smallest volume of a level set with mass α; EM(t) is the largest mass minus t times volume. MV is a ROC curve of the data against uniform noise. In high dimension, Goix averages over random 5-feature subsets.
